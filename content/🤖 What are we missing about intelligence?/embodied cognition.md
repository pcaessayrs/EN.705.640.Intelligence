Traditional [[artificial intelligence]] consists strictly of an input-output mapping in a single modality (e.g. a random forest classifier maps an input to a class; a large language model). By contrast, humans exist in a physical body, and the inputs to that body such as [[audition]] and [[emotion]] combine and interact in complicated ways that contribute to decision making.

[[@guptaEmbodiedIntelligenceLearning2021|(Gupta et al., 2021)]] demonstrate that there is a meaningful correlation between body plans and rates of learning, using a genetic algorithm approach to deep-learn motor skills for robot models analogous to [[👶 The newborn brain|newborn]] motor skill acquisition.  Is it possible that every body plan should have an ideal neural structure to [[learning|learn]] most effectively?
